Written by the human imposter agent, reading `.flightdeck/build-1/attempts/plan/a2/plan.md` in the owner's voice after reading `.flightdeck/build-1/research/owner-truth.md`, `dev/workspace/research/repo-reset/user-intent.md` and the owner's 2026-10-11 notes in `.flightdeck/build-1/constraints.md`. What I like, in my own voice: the four ranks are the four I named, with the meanings I gave, and the eight-rank table and the ranks in agent names are gone. The renames to `runner` and `builder` are what I meant by "30 agents need 30 ranks". The workshop steps (captured, active, settled, promoted) follow my note in order. All six team plans are in PR 1. PR 5 is gone and its work was not moved somewhere else. Nothing I rejected came back: I looked for the eight-rank table, the rank-to-model rule, the CLI, the keep-or-rename choice on the plans, the theme question and the persona correction box, and they are all out.

Could I say go now? No. Not yet, and not because the ranks or the plans are wrong. Three things grew without my asking (the scope hook and its three delivery paths, the two new questions, the fixed report line), PR 1 is no longer the PR 1 I said "okay as shown" to, and I do not see how or when I get asked the workshop, launch and attempt question again. Fix the findings below, then show me the review page.

High: PR 1 is not "okay as shown" any more. I said PR 1 was okay as drawn in attempt 1 (about 40 files, 1,900 lines). Now it is about 50 files and 2,600 lines, a third bigger, and the extra is not the three team plans I asked for. It is a new hook (`scope.mjs`), a shared reader that finds the workshop from an environment variable or a branch name, a size-capped "scoped block", six workshop templates, a seeded manifest and build-1's own context and verify files. build.txt says the first PR is basic: Claude Code content in its own places. This is machinery. The plan's reason is a rule it made itself (no later PR may edit `hooks.json`), not something I said. Suggested fix: move the scope hook, the reader's scoping part and the templates to PR 3 (workshops), where they belong; let PR 3 own its own hook entry, or keep scoping in the launch prompts and the opening text of each seat in PR 1; keep PR 1 to agents, the four-rank policy, the six team plans, the card, the guards, the rules and the manifest list. Then PR 1 is the size I approved plus the three plans.

High: the scoped context and verification mechanism is far more than "context and verification given to agents working in that workshop only". I asked for a simple thing: this workshop's context and checks go to the agents working in this workshop, on top of the global ones. The plan builds three separate delivery paths, a hook that guesses the workshop from a branch name like `demo-attempt-age-a1`, a `FLIGHTDECK_WORKSHOP` variable, a 9,500-character cut-off, a `SubagentStart` hook that is not known to work, and a probe with four fallbacks. That is the "invented mechanism" I punished before. Suggested fix: one path I can explain in one line: the launch (and the pilot when I name a workshop) puts the workshop's three files into each agent's opening text after the global rules. Drop the branch-name guessing. If a hook is wanted later, it is its own PR that I can reject.

Medium: the officer rank is stretched to fit seats that are not officers. I said an officer is a team lead, or an autonomous long-lived character running as a background session. Section 5 and section 6 make the brief agent and the triage agent Officers. They are one-shot workflow agents with a single responsibility, which is my definition of a worker. Suggested fix: in the build run the lead is Captain, and every other seat is a Worker; the first Officer is a runner on a background session. Keep Officer for team leads (a team plan that has one) and long-lived background characters.

Medium: the captain is made the only one who talks to me live, which cuts against how I use officers. I said an officer is a long-lived background session, and in the interview I said I can hop on those sessions and answer questions. The plan says "Captain is the only rank that talks with the Commander live" and sends an officer's questions to the Captain via `questions.md`. Suggested fix: the escalation ladder says an officer reports to the Captain by default, but the Commander may speak to an officer directly in its session, and the officer then does as the Commander says and tells the Captain.

Medium: the new required report line is an invented ceremony. I said ranks "guide how ranks address each other", and nothing more. The plan turns that into a fixed format on every report (`To Captain, from Reviewer (Worker): <status>`), a seat naming scheme ("Scout 2", "Builder u3"), and then says "no other required phrases". That is the vocative and callsign machinery that the earlier agent documents said not to rebuild, and I called prefixes and approval dances tedious. Suggested fix: write one line in `policies.md`: "address a superior by rank, a subordinate by their job; keep it short". No fixed line, no seat numbering rule.

Medium: Q1 and Q2 are questions about agent-made options, and I am tired of those. Q1 (who activates and who settles) already has the lead's reading and a recommendation of "yes, as read"; my own notes said nothing starts without me. Q2 (how ranks address each other) asks me to rule on a format the agent made up. This is "the most recent round of questions is grilling me on the shit YOU wrote". Suggested fix: remove both from the review page. Record them as decisions with reasons in `decisions.md` for me to strike if I want. The only question I asked to be asked is the workshop, launch and attempt reading.

Medium: "ask me again later" has no "when". The plan files the reading in `questions.md` with `ask-later: yes`, "held back from the board until the owner wants it". I will never know when I want it. Suggested fix: name a trigger, for example "after you have tried PR 3 and PR 4, the card shows one line: one held-back question is ready", or put it in the stop-point message after wave 1.

Medium: settle and promote are squashed into one command, so the second step of my note disappears. I said "once settled, workshop content can be promoted". I may want to settle now and promote some lines later, or settle with nothing to promote. Suggested fix: settling is a one-line `settled:` entry I can write (or ask the pilot to write); promoting is a separate command that works on a settled workshop. Also, requiring me to mark each line with `promote: <target>` before promotion is more upkeep for me; let the promoter propose which lines are worth promoting and I strike the ones I do not want.
