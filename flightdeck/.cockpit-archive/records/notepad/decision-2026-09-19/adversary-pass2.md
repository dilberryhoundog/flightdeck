## Standing and new

**N1 — The periodic leg is anchored to an event that has never happened in this cockpit's history. S-HIGH · C-HIGH**
Line 73 runs the currency review "at the end of a run or a launch". That is the answer to F4's restored second mode and the answer to F17's currency for local records, so two accepted findings now rest on it. The paper itself says at line 81 that "the first genuine record candidate arrives when a launch meets the harness", which concedes no launch has yet met it; M001 is cockpit setup and the auditor's four-day window contains no run or launch. A cadence tied to an event that has not yet occurred is not periodic, and `procedures.keep:2` says "on demand or periodically" as two live modes, not one live and one pending. If the first launch is months out, local records stamped now go unreviewed for months and the currency answer is nominal.
What must change: give the review a cadence that fires without a launch — session count, elapsed days, or the session-end procedure — with the run-and-launch trigger as an additional one, not the only one.

**N2 — Option F is adopted as a vehicle against a standing rule the change list does not name. S-MEDIUM-HIGH · C-HIGH**
Line 53 adopts the pilot's own memory as a vehicle. Line 41 states the objection accurately and does not resolve it: it is "machine-local, outside git, outside the cockpit and invisible to the commander's review", and "cuts against rule 1's principle that durable knowledge lives in the cockpit". `CLAUDE.md:9` is the clause: "Durable knowledge lives in the cockpit, not in a session. Anything worth remembering is written to `logs/`, `missions/`, `records/` or `notepad/` before the session ends." Adopting F means a class of durable knowledge — the pilot's own conduct corrections, which the paper says is most of the drift list — deliberately lives outside all four. This is the exact shape of F6, and F6 was answered by naming the clause and putting an either-or to the commander. The same standard should apply here and does not.
What must change: name `CLAUDE.md:9`, say what F holds that the cockpit does not, and either add an either-or or state the bound (for instance, memory holds only conduct already governed by a cockpit rule, never the governing statement itself).

**N3 — The revision drops the writer's working notes, which is better, but it contradicts the ruling as it reached me and says nothing about the change. S-MEDIUM-HIGH · C-MEDIUM-HIGH**
The ruling relayed to me closed F19 with "the writer's statement-to-source working notes are handed to the validator and filed in the notepad with the team's reports". Line 63 says the opposite and gives a good reason: the validator "opens the sources itself, in full, never the writer's extracts, because choosing the fragment is the failure being tested for." Line 88 then routes traceability recovery through re-validation rather than through notes. I think the revision is right and my own residual about scratch-stored notes falls away with it. But the commander may have been told the notes exist, and a reader of the paper cannot tell that a ruling was superseded.
What must change: one sentence saying the working-notes route was considered and dropped, and why, so the commander rules on what the paper actually does.

**N4 — The third top-line state is ruled but not defined. S-MEDIUM · C-HIGH**
The ruling names three states: Source, Validated, Stub, with stubs carrying no stamp. Line 61 defines the first two verbatim and leaves the third as "a record to land as a stub, marked" — the mark is never specified, so the discriminator that makes the stub loosening safe does not exist on the page. Line 75 writes four stubs without saying what their top line reads. Promotion is also unruled: nothing says what a stub's header becomes when a drift event turns it into a full record, and the mixed case — a record drawing on both the web and the commander's directives — still has two header forms and no rule.
What must change: give the stub line its literal text alongside the other two, say the header is replaced on promotion, and rule the mixed case in one clause.

**N5 — The manifest line now carries two loads that fail together. S-MEDIUM · C-MEDIUM-HIGH**
Line 69 makes commander approval the only thing limiting manifest growth, after withdrawing the invented forty. Line 65 and line 85 make the same approval the one check in the whole method with a commander-visible input. So a single act absorbs both the growth control and the sole external control, and line 93 then makes overruling a manifest line the only measure of whether vehicle classification is reliable — three jobs, one act. If the commander approves manifest lines in batches, or waves them through as routine, all three fail silently and at once, and nothing in the paper would show it.
What must change: separate at least the growth limit from the approval — a stated convention the periodic review sweeps is enough — or say plainly that the method has one control and it is this.

**N6 — The delivery hypothesis is measured entirely by the party on trial. S-MEDIUM · C-MEDIUM-HIGH**
Line 48 is the paper's most honest passage and its measure is "the pilot logs each time a trigger line sent it to a file, and any drift event a manifest line should have caught counts against it". Both halves are the pilot's own reporting and the pilot's own judgement, and the same paragraph has just established that session logs "record what happened, not what was read". A positive entry is possible where an absence is not, so the test can register successes and cannot cleanly register failures. Line 85 already concedes this class of problem; the measure that carries the recommendation's one open claim should be listed there too.
What must change: name a failure condition someone other than the pilot can observe — the commander's correction being costly is the signal the paper already trusts — or move this measure into the self-assessment paragraph with the rest.

**N7 — Adopting F and G leaves the same fact in three stores with no rule about which governs. S-LOW-MEDIUM · C-MEDIUM**
After line 81 the branch rule sits in the guard, in `commander.md:17`, and in the pilot's auto memory, which line 41 already notes "duplicates the commander's dossier, where the same branch rule already lives". `CLAUDE.md:31`, rule 9, says "Refine structures rather than pile onto them", and memory.md warns that contradicting rules across files make the model "pick one arbitrarily". Adding vehicles without a de-duplication rule is how that happens.
What must change: one line in the vehicle test saying a fact lives in one vehicle, which one governs when it is already elsewhere, and that the others are retired or reduced to a pointer.

**N8 — "Structural" is a pilot judgement and is missing from the self-assessment list. S-LOW · C-HIGH**
Line 65 checks a procedure "by the mini-adversary roster only when structural". Nothing defines structural, and the pilot decides it about its own document, so the one validation procedures get is gated by the author. Line 85's list of self-assessed items — drift, vehicle, log, briefs — should include it.

**N9 — The stamp still cannot resolve to a team. S-LOW · C-MEDIUM**
Line 61 routes recovery through "crew rule 7... findable by the stamp's date", and the example line names the seat `source-validator` and a date. Seat names recur across flights and `crew.json` holds one row per seat, so two teams stamping on one day are not separable. A team id is one short token and is the cockpit's own unit of record under crew rule 7. I raised this before and accept it may be below the commander's threshold; it costs one token to close and I would close it.
