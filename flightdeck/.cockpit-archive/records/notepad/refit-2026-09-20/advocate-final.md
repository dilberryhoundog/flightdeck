## What he asked for that is missing or different, ranked

**1. Half of "defined shape" is not built: the body.** His [Or056]: "The whole purpose of having some schemas around is so that our documents have a defined shape, not just conjured up each time an agent writes one; it is so a new session can write a document the same way you write a document." What exists: 18 schemas that describe frontmatter only. `base/verify/README.md` line 11: "Everything after the closing `---` is the body, which the linter does not read." A new session now gets the metadata of a dossier exactly right and still invents its headings, its section order and its length from nothing — the conjuring he named. Mitigated by each schema's per-field `description`, which does teach. Cure: one `x-sections` list per schema naming the required headings, checked by heading text; it is a small addition to a linter that already reads the file.

**2. The corpus-wide run is 171 faults, and 170 of them are outside the scope he set.** His [frontmatter.txt]: "Documents to receive frontmatter: ID based: Work, Commander Desk; Crew: Crew, Officers." The linter faults every markdown file that has no `type`, so a run over the cockpit reports `CLAUDE.md`, 21 READMEs, `work/procedures/triggers.md`, 6 session logs — which this refit's own constraint forbids editing — and 134 notepad scratch files that rule 3 declares scratch. Against [Or054] "whatever saves the most tokens and reduces churn" and "small output on success", the honest state of the cockpit is the scoped run's 1 fault in 244 files; the pilot who runs the obvious command sees 171 lines of noise and learns to stop running it. Cure: an untyped markdown file is a fault only inside the four families he named; elsewhere it is silence, or one summary line behind a flag.

**3. Manifest rows were machine-truncated where they should have been written.** His [json-data-consistency.txt]: "The manifest records the collection of units. token efficient, enough data for unit disclosure. Flow = agent needs context --> manifest --> unit." In `MANIFEST-orders.json` 20 of 58 rows end mid-word in an ellipsis, and in `MANIFEST-dispatches.json` 10 of 14 do; Or058's row reads "…node should work, it works" and stops. A row cut off at a character count does not disclose its unit, so the agent opens the unit anyway and the flow he drew pays for itself twice. Every other manifest (decisions, dossiers, requests, crew, workshop, sparks) carries a written field and is fine. Cure: one written line per row for orders and dispatches.

**4. Two manifests were renamed rather than reshaped.** His [json-data-consistency.txt] opens: "Create consistency in json data." Thirteen of the fifteen manifests hold their rows in `units` and point with `path`. `records/logs/MANIFEST-logs.json` holds `logs` and points with `file`; `records/logs/MANIFEST-branches.json` holds `branches` and points at nothing. These are the old `index.json` and `branch-manifest.json` given the new name and left in the old shape, which is the one thing his naming rule exists to prevent. `records/logs/crew-manifest.json` is the same hazard in reverse — a file named like a manifest that is not one, and his [Or051] complaint was exactly that "`orders.json` could be a manifest file but it could also be something else". It is frozen history, so the cure is its name, not its contents.

**5. The fault line dropped its line number.** His [Or054]: "on error, enough guidance to fix directly without a tool call, a small edit to fix (preventing a large write)." The lines name the file, the field, the value and the pattern — `stamp[0] is "20-09-2026", which does not match ^[0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}-[0-9]{2}$` — which is good, and a negative test confirmed each of the four fault kinds fires. But there is no line number, where the stage A format he was shown had one, so on a long document the writer still opens it to find the key. Cure: carry the line through; the reader already knows it.

**6. Manuals have no manifest.** `base/verify/schema/manifest.json` claims the collection word `manuals` in its `x-select`, and no `MANIFEST-manuals.json` exists; `records/manuals/` is indexed by README alone while every other collection got a manifest. Either the word should go or the file should be written.

**7. Two numbering choices were settled for him, quietly.** [Or057] says only "Prefixes is wrong on `P`: solely for procedures", and his request example was [json-data-consistency.txt] "eg Rq001, Rq002". Procedures are now P001 to P006 renumbered from the old P101 plan, and requests kept their old numbers, so Rq004 is the fourth request but the first one he ever saw. Both are defensible; neither is his, and both are now in files and in the `CLAUDE.md` table. The `stamp` shape — three items, date, who, session — is the pilot's too: [Or051] ruled only that "'recorded' was the initial form of 'stamp', renamed to avoid confusion with the records room."

## What was added beyond his words

- Manuals carry frontmatter (`type`, `source`, `style`, `stamp`), though his list of documents to receive it names only Work, Commander Desk, Crew and Officers. It serves his searchability line, and it does keep the old header line as keys, but he did not ask for it.
- Per-type extra fields with no source in his words: `branch` and `assigned_by` on a mission, `trigger` and `approved` on a procedure, `room`, `agent_type` and `model` on a crew dossier, `source` and `style` on a manual. His [frontmatter.txt] line 1 is "Fields are consistent across document types"; the shared five are consistent, and this is variety layered on top.
- Schemas for eight types he never named (log index, branch register, topic, roster, officer dossier, dispatch, manifest, spark). Licensed by [Or057] "Schemas for all document types", and cheap: one file each.
- `manifest.json` lists all fourteen collection words in one `x-select`, so a new collection costs two edits (its schema and that list) rather than the one the README promises.
- Size, measured: 333 lines of linter plus 331 lines of `lib/` copied from flightcrew with their origin stated, and 18 schemas at 40K. Against "basic linter" and "minimal changes" that is modest, half of it is not new code, and Or058's "mirror the flightdeck one; node should work" was followed exactly. Adding a document type costs one file; moving a room costs nothing.

## Verdict

Decent, by his own standard in Or057 — this is the thing he advised, built where he said to put it, and the cockpit now matches his sketch room for room.

The gap that matters is that a schema still says only how a document is labelled, not how it is written, so his sentence about a new session writing as the last one did is delivered for the frontmatter and not for the page.

Three things he should look at himself, in this order: (1) `commander/desk/in/dossiers/Ds001.md` and `records/manuals/claude-code/agent-teams.md`, the two frontmatter blocks he will see most, to say whether that is what he meant; (2) run `base/verify/cockpit-lint` over the whole cockpit himself and rule whether 171 faults is the linter working or crying wolf, because that one call decides whether the pilot keeps using it; (3) whether a schema should also name a document's sections, which is finding 1 and the only piece of his purpose still outstanding.
