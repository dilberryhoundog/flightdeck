## Topic 1 — the axes

### The six, each with a clause two readers can apply alike

- **Speed** — the score is **relay rounds to settle**, with parallel width and the longest dependency chain as the two structural facts that predict it. Pass 7 added "a stated pace or friction fact in the dispatch's own text" as a fourth item with no rule for combining the four; that was free-text judgement wearing a measure's clothes and is dropped (A6).
- **Coverage** — scored against a **pre-registered denominator**: the parts of the question enumerated in the question file at dispatch and frozen there. It is a pilot artefact, so it scores the pilot's decomposition and not the question, and is therefore attacked at the formation-gate step by `commander-advocate` **before the working seats are briefed** — the advocate is itself briefed at the gate, so "before any seat is briefed" excluded the seat doing the attacking (A5).
- **Quality** — schema and lint outcomes, and **the pilot's first commit of the team's output against what lands after it**. Pass 7 said "pilot repair commits after the team's commit", naming an event the cockpit forbids: crew do not commit, and T014 and T015 both scope "No commits by crew" (A2).
- **Alignment** — rulings dated **between the team landing and its dossier being approved**, in both this topic and the Maintenance section, which pass 7 defined two different ways, the looser one counting every later ruling in the cockpit against every team (A7). Plus rework caused by a commander answer, and whether a seat's stated reason names his advice, order, idea or vision.
- **Security** — the harness enforces the core: permission prompts surface in the lead's session, "A teammate can't approve a permission prompt or supply consent on your behalf, and a teammate that was denied an action can't relay it to another teammate to bypass the check", and a relayed approval claim is "untrusted input rather than confirmation from you". Scored before flight from the briefs — does each state a read and a write boundary, how wide is the write boundary, does any seat take content from outside the cockpit — and after flight from `git show --stat` of **the pilot's commit of the team's output** against `scope`, once `scope` is required. "Can any seat commit" is struck from the clause for the same reason as A2: none can.
- **Adversarial** — the commander's definition (Or066: "adversarial is the axis name, how well does the team review itself"). Scored from whether a seat's only product is findings against another seat's work, whether the attacked pass was frozen and numbered, and defects caught in-team against caught after landing, **the in-flight side read from the dispatch `outcome` field where catch events land when they are recorded at all** — `outcome` is optional in the schema and two of the clearest catches sit in notepad files instead, so it must be made required beside `scope` (A4). **Findings withdrawn are not counted flat**: the record files withdrawal as the adversary working, not failing, so a ratio as pass 7 wrote it would reward an adversary that never concedes (A3). Withdrawals carry a one-word reason code, which is F64's repair and costs nothing.

### Multiply attackers; do not multiply judges

**Adversarial investigation:** multiplicity is the point, since anchoring is what is defeated — teammates made "explicitly adversarial: each one's job is not only to investigate its own theory but to challenge the others'". **Judging against a rubric:** "a single LLM call with a single prompt outputting scores from 0.0-1.0 and a pass-fail grade was the most consistent and aligned with human judgements" — which compares one judge call against several *per component*, so it is evidence for one rubric-driven judge, not for none (B11). Pass 3's claim that the cockpit's five attack seats are mostly judges is withdrawn, and pass 7's replacement ratio goes with it: the audit itself retired the denominator by finding that one of the five is not an attack seat at all, and "roughly three to two" over five integers was not a count (A1). What stands without a ratio: `commander-advocate` and `adversary` investigate, `alignment-judge` compares, and no two of the five have been shown to return the same finding on the same file, which is the test a merge needs.

### The formation gate, which is not an axis

Task-shape fit is a gate applied before a team exists, not a seventh column scored on teams that already exist: "most coding tasks involve fewer truly parallelizable tasks than research, and LLM agents are not yet great at coordinating and delegating to other agents in real time." It is step one of the Setup procedure, and where the coverage parts list is attacked.

## Topic 2 — the team types

**The four types are defined over teammates** (A11). A dispatch flown as ordinary subagents — T007 is one — is not one of the four; it is a seat or two without a team, and the paper takes no position on naming that. Teammate teams need `CLAUDE_CODE_EXPERIMENTAL_AGENT_TEAMS`, and the two keys in play differ by precedence: user settings set it to "0" while `base/settings/pilot.settings.json` sets it to "1" and the launch flag wins for that key, whereas the todo-tools key exists only in user settings and therefore is not overridden.

**Autonomous** — runs to a finish and reports, **and no Autonomous team appears anywhere in T001 to T017, so everything said of it here is predicted rather than observed** (A15). Teammates inherit the lead's permission mode except `dontAsk`; skip-permissions on the lead applies to all. It works under a lead in `auto` mode while work stays inside classifier auto-approval, subject to a budget: twenty blocks in a session, plus a stall at three in a row — and "any allowed action resets the consecutive counter, while the total counter persists for the session", so the gauge is the twenty and the three is a stall any allowed action clears (F65's refinement). Spawning teammates "requires an interactive session", so a headless run gets ordinary subagents. Task status can lag and block dependents, and F35 only half answers it: the harness "auto-unblocks once completion is marked", and failing to mark completion is precisely what lags (A8), so the remedy rests on the `TeammateIdle` hook alone. The pooled-queue test confirms the working half — task #6 became claimable the moment the last of its three dependencies was marked complete, never before.

**Pilot leader** — the pilot relays and rules between rounds. `/resume` and `/rewind` "do not restore in-process teammates", so a resumed lead keeps its own context and the files on disk, but no teammate (A13).

**Commander in the room** — he answers through the pilot's seat, as in T010 and this flight, the pilot holding the paper, relaying every ruling and recording each as an order.

**Commander at the seat.** He does occupy it: "plain text and skills go to that teammate, but built-in commands still run in the lead's session", and Escape "interrupts that teammate's current turn"; bounded by built-in commands and `/model` and `/fast` staying the lead's, and by "Lead is fixed". The idle notification still reaches the lead, and the exchange is on disk since a teammate's transcript is readable there. **Inferred, not documented** (A12): that the seat can tell his words from a relay rests on a sentence sited in the Permissions section about consent laundering, while the same source records that nothing documents the lead observing a direct exchange. It is measurable in one step — ask a teammate what its inbound message was labelled — and should be measured before the filing step leans on it. The filing step itself stands on the narrow ground: a ruling given at a seat never lands in `commander/orders/`, where the cockpit looks.

**Cross-constraints.** Commander in the room is strongest on alignment and slowest; T010 took eleven passes. Commander at the seat is that type with the relay removed and a filing duty added. **Pass 7 said this buys back the speed M10 shows relay costs; that is withdrawn** — M10 attributes its four crossings and three extra passes to the pilot relaying live "instead of freezing the pass under attack", so the cost is the absent freeze, and removing the relay while keeping live delivery reproduces it (A10). The gain from the fourth type is a round-trip, and the record does not price it. **Pilot leader is the only type where the pilot's judgement, rather than his relay, is the ruling** (A9).

**The name.** **Commander at the seat**, on the set's grammar of position: Autonomous (nobody), Pilot leader (the pilot), Commander in the room (through the pilot), Commander at the seat (direct). "Direct interview" is the most legible standalone name and the one that breaks the set.

## Maintenance: harvesting and the improvement test

**Measured from files**, the officially preferred method: code-based grading first, "Fastest and most reliable, extremely scalable"; human second, "Avoid if possible"; LLM-based third. Alignment: rulings in the bounded window. Adversarial: defects caught in-team against after landing, the in-flight side from `outcome`, withdrawals by reason code. Speed: relay rounds to settle, and dispatch date to `status: finished`. Coverage: parts of the frozen list with no seat accountable. Quality: lint and schema failures, and what follows the pilot's first commit of the team's output. Security: the brief-side clause before flight, `git show --stat` after, once `scope` is required.
