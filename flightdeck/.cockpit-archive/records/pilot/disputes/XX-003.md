# XX-003 — Who extracts the commander's words, an agent or the pilot

Both sides come out of `cockpit.constitution` lines 32 and 38, which describe the same act of extraction.

## CA-008

"If written internally they stay frozen inside the document as the commander wrote them, but also immediately extracted into the adjacent commander record corpus, by an agent who rewrites them in the language and format required." [32]

## CO-097

"However if the commander attaches a record type (`CA:`, `CO:`, `CQ:`), this is immediately extracted to the correct record location by the pilot, which may or may not change the direction of the conversation." [38]

## The contradiction

CA-008 gives the extraction to an agent, who rewrites the commander's words into the language and format the record needs. CO-097 gives the same act to the pilot, directly. The two are close enough that they may have been meant as one instruction, but they name different hands, and which hand it is decides how the cockpit runs: line 61 holds the pilot's writing to their own records, the commander's records on their behalf, and a few other places, and CO-102 puts heavy constraints on the pilot so that team usage comes first and the context window stays light. If every attached `CA:`, `CO:` or `CQ:` is extracted by the pilot in the session, the pilot is doing clerical reading and writing that the constitution elsewhere pushes onto crew. A settlement that says the pilot files the record and a clerk does the reading, or the reverse, would answer it.
