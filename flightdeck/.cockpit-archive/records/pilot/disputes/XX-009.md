# XX-009 — An order lasts one session, and every order still binds

Both sides come out of `cockpit.constitution` lines 27 and 40.

## CA-006

"[CO] Commander order are the pilot's conduct (actions and behaviour) for the session. Orders are promoted to standing orders [SO] if they are universal conduct applicable across any sessions." [27]

## CO-099 and CA-011

"The pilot is bound to all existing commander records available at the time." [40]

"Commanders records are the source of truth." [40]

## The contradiction

CA-006 scopes an order to the session it was given in, and reserves conduct that applies across sessions for a standing order, which is what promotion to SO is for. CO-099 binds the pilot to all existing commander records available at the time, and CA-011 calls those records the source of truth; an order that has not been retired is still an existing record. So a plain CO from a past session is both expired and binding. The question is live rather than theoretical: the corpus migration is filing CO-001 to CO-092 from past sessions, and if CA-006 governs, almost none of them bind the next session and the corpus is history rather than law until each is promoted. If CO-099 governs, promotion to SO marks nothing that is not already true and the difference between a CO and an SO is only a label. A settlement would say what an unpromoted order from a past session does: bind until retired, lapse at the end of its session, or bind only where a later record has not replaced it.

# Commander Response

CA:{Extracted: CA-051} All commander orders are recorded in a session. If a session fails or ends, the CO is still valid until it is 'settled' in a later session. The pilot settles an order by having a fully approved chain of resulting records and moves the record to settled/. The commander can request it opened again should they want. When a CO can be; never settled or settled repeatedly, it is a good candidate for an SO. The commander may choose to retire a CO that is stale. The pilot can prompt the commander if they also feel a CO has gone stale.
