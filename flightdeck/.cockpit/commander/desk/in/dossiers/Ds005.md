# Ds005 — The procedures room: scaffold and five starting procedures

## The room in one paragraph

The room is built as a scaffold and nothing in it is live. A procedure is a pre-recorded routine that matches a roster with tasks, context files and steps, so repeated work is not re-prompted and does not vary between sessions. Two branches: `cockpit/` for the pilot's own conduct, `flightcrew/` for conducting a launch, which holds only a README until the cockpit fitout settles. Five cockpit procedures are written as drafts, sixteen to eighteen lines each. Until you approve one it is headed `Draft:` and is not followed, and the manifest is not imported into `CLAUDE.md`.

## The shape

1. **The file.** Title; one header line; Trigger; Roster, or "pilot alone"; Tasks, one line per seat saying what it does and returns; Context files; numbered Steps; Leaves behind (the log line and files touched). Fifty lines at most. A procedure points at the roster, file or rule that owns a fact and restates none of them.
2. **Authority.** The header reads `Approved: commander, <date>.` once you approve it, and that line in the file is the authority; the index mirrors it. An edit to the steps returns the file to draft.
3. **Your three trigger kinds.** On demand: the manifest line states a condition ("before forming a team"), never a summary. Named: no condition and no event, invoked by name; every procedure can also be called by its name. The Named list starts empty in the cockpit; "launch ending" will be its first entry when the flightcrew branch is built. Periodical: due on an event, never a date.
4. **Due without machinery.** The pilot appends each mission phase close and launch close, dated, to `MANIFEST-procedures.json`. A periodical is due when the latest such event is later than its last run. The limit: a missed run shows at the next look at the index, not by itself. A run on your order is just a run.
5. **The manifest.** `work/procedures/triggers.md`, one line per approved procedure under its kind, imported into `CLAUDE.md`. It routes and explains nothing.
6. **Scripts** stay in `base/bin/`; a procedure may call one by path. Session start and end are not here; they go to the agent body.
7. **One rule to stop a collision.** Ds004 says no document is written ahead of a drift event. That governs knowledge. A routine you name is ordered, not forecast, and the room's README says so.
