# T010 exploration paper — rooms, identifiers, JSON shape, frontmatter

Held by `option-maker`. Mission M001, team T010, dispatched by `pilot` (Ace), 2026-09-20. Pass 2: the auditor's and scout's reports folded in. Not a design, not a refactor plan. The commander's four files are ideas, not orders.

## The finding that reorders everything else

The commander renamed three files on the desk, `out-advice/DS001.md` to `CA001.md` and its two siblings. The auditor counted what that left behind: **at least 47 stale references across 21-plus files**, in `commanders-desk/`, `base/proposals.json`, `base/decisions.json`, `quarters/commander/orders.json`, twenty references across nine `logs/topics/*.json`, and fourteen notepad files. One of them, `logs/topics/launch-and-run.json:13`, is a `superseded_by` field, so it is load-bearing metadata and not prose. There is no link checker in the cockpit; nothing found or fixed any of these except the grep run for that report. And per `notepad/adversary-2026-09-18/fit-critic.md:24` this is the **second** time this one room has done it — the first was `notepad/commanders-advice/` to `commanders-desk/out-advice/`.

I re-counted independently and got **89** line references, higher than the auditor's conservative 47. But the raw number is the wrong number, and splitting it by zone changes the recommendation rather than merely strengthening it:

- `commanders-desk/` 9 and `base/` 12 — **21 in live authoritative files.** These are broken and must be repaired.
- `logs/topics/` 33 — a live index of the commander's statements. Should be repaired, with the caveat below.
- `notepad/` 34 — scratch. Cockpit rule 3 says nothing there is authoritative, so repairing it is mostly wasted work.
- `quarters/commander/orders.json` 1 and one session log — **these must NOT be repaired.** The order is the commander's verbatim text and the log is frozen history. Both record what was true when written; rewriting them falsifies the record.

So the honest figure is 21 genuinely broken references from a three-file rename, not 89. That is still damning — but the split carries a finding the raw count hides: **a checker that repaired every match would corrupt a verbatim order and a session log.** Any reference tooling has to know which rooms are authoritative, which are scratch, and which are frozen. That is a constraint on the checker's design, and it is the kind of thing that only shows up when the numbers are taken apart.

Scaled up: the auditor's inbound counts put `records/` at 168 occurrences across 47 files and `notepad/` at 160 across 56. A room regroup is this same failure two orders of magnitude larger, and identifier renames are it again.

So the real first question is not which scheme. It is whether a zone-aware reference checker exists before anything moves. Every option below is priced on the assumption it does not, and every one gets dramatically cheaper if it does. **Build the checker first** is the one recommendation in this paper that does not depend on how the commander answers anything.

## Area 1 — Rooms

The idea: regroup ten top-level rooms into five or six (`base/`, `commander/`, `records/`, `work/`, `team/`), because fitout is causing the cockpit to settle differently than intended. The problem is real and the auditor located it precisely: rooms were added one at a time, so siblings sit at different altitudes, and the canonical map in CLAUDE.md has already fallen out of step with the disk.

**R0 — leave it as it is.** Buys: no migration, no reference churn. Costs: the drift stands and the top level flattens further with each new room. Breaks: nothing.

**R1 — the regroup as drawn.** Buys: rooms grouped by purpose rather than by the date they were needed. Costs: on the auditor's counts, several hundred stale references with no repair path — `records/` alone is 168 occurrences across 47 files — plus the eleven script paths. Breaks: four things. (a) `records/` is redrawn to mean "anything recorded" while today's records become `manuals/`; rule 2, `records/README.md` and the commander's own CA001 distinction all rest on the current meaning, and the word would mean two things across the `dev/branches/` archives. (b) `dispatch/` moves under `records/`, but rule 10 keeps dispatch entries current as work happens — they are live state, not records. (c) the commander appears twice in the sketch, at top level and under `team/officers/`. (d) **the sketch has no notepad room at all**, and the notepad is the largest room in the cockpit at 113 files; that is either an oversight or a decision about the notepad that the sketch does not state.

**R2 — drift repair only.** Fix the phantom `base/dossiers/` in the map, put the proposals index with its units or say why not, finish the CA rename's 47 references, update `commanders-desk/README.md`, which still documents the old `DS###.md` filenames. Buys: removes the observed damage for a handful of edits. Costs: leaves the altitude problem. Breaks: nothing.

**R3 — regroup where it is cheap, keep the loaded names.** R2, plus `work/` over missions and workshop, and leave `records/`, `quarters/`, `logs/`, `dispatch/`, `base/`, `notepad/` and `commanders-desk/` named as they are. The auditor's counts make this the cheap corner of the sketch by measurement, not taste: workshop is 22 occurrences and procedures 42, the two cheapest rooms to move, against records at 168. Buys: most of the altitude fix for about a fortieth of the churn, and it keeps a word the commander has spent three sessions loading with meaning. Costs: the top level goes to eight, not six. Breaks: nothing that a grep cannot finish.

**R4 — make the map self-maintaining, whatever the layout.** Orthogonal; combines with any of R0 to R3. A per-room `CLAUDE.md`, which the scout confirms is lazy — it enters context only when an agent reads a file in that room, so it is free at launch — and a checker that fails when a cited path does not resolve or a room has no README. Buys: it fixes the cause rather than the symptom, and it is the only option that makes any of the others safe to attempt. Costs: one script. Breaks: nothing. Correction to pass 1: I offered `@` imports as an alternative here. The scout has shown imports load at launch and defer nothing, so an import-based map buys organisation only. Subdirectory CLAUDE.md is the mechanism that actually works.

**Leaning: R4 first, then R2, then R3.** The measured defect is drift between map and disk plus the absence of any reference repair; R4 addresses both and R1 without R4 is the 47-reference failure at scale. Wrong if the commander wants the sketch as the target shape rather than a starting point and is content to pay the reference cost by hand, or if the orphan-branch move is near, in which case regrouping before the quickfire commit paths are scripted is cheaper than after.

## Area 2 — Identifiers

The idea: one shape, `<1-3 capitals><3 digits>`; single capital for common units, two or three for combined words; and rename `desk/proposals` to `desk/requests` so `Rq` frees `P` for procedures. The problem is real: ten numbered registers exist with no stated rule, so the pilot has no way to know what the eleventh should be called.

**I0 — leave it as it is.** Buys: nothing changes. Costs: the next kind is invented ad hoc again, as `CA` just was, without even a register. Breaks: nothing.

**I1 — the shape with the collisions resolved as sketched** (P to procedures, proposals to `Rq`, dossiers to `Ds`). Buys: one rule stated once. Costs: 213 `P` references and twelve filenames, 369 `DS` references, with no repair path and a 47-reference precedent for a three-file rename. Breaks: three things. (a) `Ds001.md` and `DS001.md` are the same path on this machine's case-insensitive filesystem, so a partial rename silently overwrites; Jira independently enforces `[A-Z][A-Z]+` for the same class of reason. (b) It breaks the commander's own capitals rule, stated in the same file. (c) It violates the one identifier principle the scout found holding across ADR, RFC and Jira alike: a prefix is reserved for one kind and never reassigned. `P` has twelve live proposals. Reassigning it to procedures is precisely the move none of the three conventions permits.

**I2 — the shape, collisions resolved the other way.** Keep `P` = proposal and `DS` = dossier, the incumbents by 213 and 369 references, and give procedures something else or nothing. Buys: the rule without the rename, and it is what ADR, RFC and Jira would all do. Costs: `P` for procedure was the commander's preference and is lost. Breaks: nothing.

**I3 — two tiers, stated.** Numbers for things counted, appended to and cited across rooms (M, S, W, P, DS, CA, D, O, T, C); names for things cited by name and never counted (rosters, procedures, branches, crew seats). Buys: it describes what the cockpit already does in three of its registers and what its documents read better for — `forming-a-team` is a better citation in a brief than `P002` — and it gives crew dossiers something to put in a frontmatter field, which numbers cannot. Costs: two rules instead of one. Breaks: nothing; it makes the status quo explicit.

**I4 — enforce it.** A checker that mints the next number for a kind, enforces uppercase and three digits, refuses a prefix already taken, and verifies every reference resolves. Buys: the same script as R4, and it is what would have caught all 47 stale references. Costs: one script. Breaks: nothing.

**Leaning: I3 + I2 + I4.** Keep `P` and `DS`, name the things already named, give `CA` the register it lacks, write the two-tier rule into CLAUDE.md once, and let the checker hold it. Wrong if the commander wants procedures cited by number in rosters and briefs, in which case `Pr` is the cheap answer and taking `P` is not.

## Area 3 — Manifest and unit JSON

The idea: a lightweight named manifest per room, recognisable in streaming output, pointing at per-unit files, so an agent reads manifest then unit. The auditor's survey changes this question's shape entirely: **the cockpit already does this in nine of about seventeen substantive JSON files.** The commander is not proposing a new pattern. The commander is describing the cockpit's existing majority pattern and asking for it to be finished and named.

**J0 — leave it as it is.** Buys: nothing to do. Costs: `cockpit.json` and `orders.json` sit at about 5.5k tokens each and grow by one entry per team and per session forever. Breaks: nothing yet.

**J1 — split everywhere, with the `MANIFEST-` filename.** Buys: uniformity and the streaming recognisability the commander wants. Costs: the four remaining candidates are worth splitting; the rest are not, and `rosters.json` (450 tokens of seat-name lists) and `missions.json` (324 tokens, two missions) would become file sprawl for nothing. The rename also touches nine manifests whose names already say what they are, two of which `session-start.sh` reads directly.

**J2 — finish the pattern in the four files that need it.** `dispatch/cockpit.json`, `orders.json`, `decisions.json`, `workshop.json`. Buys: the actual benefit, in the rooms that actually have it, following a pattern proven nine times in-house. Costs: none beyond the moves. Breaks: nothing. Note `workshop.json` already has a documented file-per-item escape hatch that has never been used — splitting it is less a change than an instruction to start using what the README already permits.

**J3 — state the threshold, enforce it.** A manifest splits when it passes a stated size. The scout supplies a defensible number rather than a guessed one: Anthropic's own auto-memory index is capped at 200 lines or 25KB, past which the content is dropped on load. Buys: the decision is made once, and the currency-review procedure or the checker flags files that have crossed it. Costs: any threshold will be argued with.

**J4 — do not split, shrink.** Move prose out of JSON rows into the markdown unit that in most cases already exists. Buys: much of the saving with no new files. Costs: no help to `orders.json`, which is prose by nature.

**On the `MANIFEST-` prefix.** The stated reason is recognisability in streaming output. `dossiers.json`, `topics.json` and `missions.json` already carry it. `MANIFEST-dispatch-cockpit.json` additionally encodes its room in its filename, which means any room move renames every manifest and re-couples area 1 to area 3 for no benefit. If recognisability is the whole aim, the existing names have it.

**On unit filenames.** The sketch writes units as `T001.json` and `Or001.json`. The cockpit's own existing units are `DS001-flightcrew-recon.md`, `M001-cockpit-setup.md`, `S009-self-sustaining-cockpit.md` — id plus slug, which is both sortable and readable in a file listing. The scout notes Anthropic's own memory example favours descriptive names over codes, and that no official guidance backs opaque IDs. Keeping id-plus-slug is free and strictly better.

**Leaning: J3 with J2 as its first application, id-plus-slug filenames, and reject the `MANIFEST-<room>` rename.** Wrong if units need to be individually addressable for a reason other than token cost — an orphan-branch worktree checking out one unit, for instance.

## Area 4 — Frontmatter

The idea: consistent YAML frontmatter on generated markdown, replacing bolded metadata, for searchability across the corpus, with a line in CLAUDE.md stating the convention. The problem is real: DS001 carries the same facts three times over, and P004 reached the desk with two frontmatter fields empty while `proposals.json` carries a populated `status` for the same proposal. The two conventions are already disagreeing.

**One fact must be stated before the options.** The scout searched the harness documentation: frontmatter is parsed only in subagents, slash commands, skills, path-scoped rules, output styles and auto-memory. **A dossier read with the Read tool is none of those, so its frontmatter is inert to the harness.** The whole case for frontmatter therefore rests on grep and on the pilot's own stated convention. That is a legitimate case — it is exactly what `MEMORY.md` does — but it means frontmatter buys nothing automatically, and a convention no script enforces is the convention that produced the 47 stale references.

**F0 — leave the bolded blocks.** Buys: nothing to do. Costs: the three-way duplication stands. Breaks: nothing.

**F1 — uniform frontmatter replacing the bolded blocks.** Buys: `grep '^unit: "DS001"'` and `grep '^type: "Dossier"'` work across 78 files; the bolded duplicate goes. Costs: the facts then live in frontmatter and in the manifest row with no stated owner — the same defect one layer up. Breaks: the commander's own example, which must become `["P004", "P005"]` or a block list.

**F2 — the markdown owns; the manifest is generated from it.** Buys: one owner, and a unit file complete on its own, which matters if files are read outside the cockpit. Costs: a generator, and a manifest nobody may hand-edit, which rule 10 invites the pilot to do anyway.

**F3 — the manifest owns; frontmatter carries `type` and `unit` only,** as CA001 already does. Buys: duplication impossible, and it is the cheapest. Costs: a file handed to crew by path — which is how the crew protocol briefs crew — arrives with no date, status or lineage.

**F4 — fields, plus a checker that compares them to the manifest row.** Buys: duplication allowed but cannot drift. Costs: two sources a human still has to reconcile.

**F5 — split ownership by the kind of fact.** The markdown owns what a reader opening it alone must have: status, date, approval, lineage. The manifest owns what belongs to the collection: which units exist, their order, cross-links, paths. Neither restates the other. Buys: a rule rather than a side, and it is what two rooms already do unnamed — `procedures/README.md` says the file's Approved line is authoritative and the index mirrors it, and rule 2 puts the record's source line on the record. Costs: every field is classified once, and title and mission will be argued.

**Where my pass-1 leaning was wrong.** I leant F3. Then I found the cockpit has already ruled the other way in two rooms, and F3 would overturn a stated convention to fix a defect in a third. Withdrawn.

**Fields, for whichever wins.** All types: `type`, `unit`. ID-based work documents: `work` (mission), `context` (what it came from), a date. Type-specific: `status` and `recorded` for proposals, `generates` for dossiers, session and author for dossiers. Crew and officer documents share none of these and have no identifier at all, so they need `seat: "explorer"`, or area 2's named tier has to make a name a legitimate `unit` value. Until one of those is settled, the commander's "Crew: Crew, Officers" line cannot be executed as written.

**Leaning: F5 with F1's mechanics.** Uniform frontmatter as asked, bolded blocks removed, and the ownership rule stated so the fields cannot be duplicated into the manifest. This fixes the DS001 triple by deleting the block rather than the frontmatter, keeps the two conventions the cockpit already has, and survives a crew member handed one file by path. Wrong if the commander wants the manifest to be the single readable surface and the markdown to be pure prose, in which case F3 is right and the procedures and records rooms should change to match rather than be preserved.

## How the areas constrain each other

The pilot's working view is that the first three ideas are one scheme. Tested: partly.

- **Identifiers and unit JSON are one decision.** Unit files are named for their IDs, so splitting before the prefix set is fixed means renaming every unit file — and renaming has no repair path.
- **Frontmatter is downstream of identifiers, and more tightly than it looks.** It needs the prefix set for `unit:`, and for crew and officer documents it needs the *named* tier to exist at all. If area 2 rules "numbers only", a large part of the commander's own frontmatter scope has nothing to put in the field.
- **Rooms are separable, once two threads are cut.** Only two things bind rooms to the rest: manifest filenames that encode the room, and stored paths that cross rooms with `../`. Drop the room name from manifest filenames, make every stored path cockpit-relative, and rooms decouple. Both are cheap and worth doing whichever option wins.
- **One script spans all four, and it is the precondition for any of them.** IDs, frontmatter, manifest-to-unit resolution, room READMEs and cross-file references are all the same checker. Its absence is what turned a three-file rename into 47 broken references. If it is built, the enforced option becomes the leaning in every area.

## Order of change

1. **The reference checker, before anything moves.** It is the only item here that is unconditional, it makes every later step cheap, and without it each later step repeats a failure the cockpit has now had twice.
2. **Then the free repairs:** the phantom `base/dossiers/` map line, the 21 live CA references (not the scratch ones, and never the verbatim order or the session log), the `commanders-desk/README.md` filename pattern, DS001's invalid YAML, the room name out of manifest filenames, every stored path cockpit-relative.
3. **Then, together:** the prefix set and the two-tier question (area 2), and the ownership ruling (area 4's F2/F3/F5 fork). Everything downstream cites the prefixes, and the ownership ruling decides whether frontmatter grows fields or stays two keys.
4. **Then, in any order, without rework:** the four unit splits and the threshold, the frontmatter field list, the rooms.
5. **Rework risk if reversed.** Renaming `P` after frontmatter lands means re-editing every `unit:` and `context:` line. Regrouping rooms after `MANIFEST-<room>.json` lands means renaming every manifest. Splitting units before the prefix set is fixed means renaming every unit file. Doing any of them before the checker means doing the grep by hand, three times.

## What would block the orphan-branch idea

Out of scope, noted only where the other three could close a door.

- **The guard's allowlist is a hardcoded repo-root-relative tuple** while `pilot.settings.json` already exports `FLIGHTDECK_COCKPIT`. In a separate worktree or orphan checkout the prefix stops matching and the guard either blocks everything or waves everything through. Reading the env var is a one-line change, free now. This is the one real blocker found.
- **Stored paths that leave the cockpit** break under an orphan checkout. Keeping every stored path cockpit-relative is the same cheap change as in area 1, for a second reason.
- **Quickfire commits keyed to locations** (`commit cockpit`, `commit launch`) assume stable top-level room names. If rooms are to be regrouped at all, doing it before those are scripted is materially cheaper than after.
- **Not a blocker:** `@procedures/triggers.md` is relative to CLAUDE.md and survives a move of the whole cockpit. Nested per-room CLAUDE.md files would too, and the scout confirms they load lazily.
