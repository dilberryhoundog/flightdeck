# fit-critic — Stage A against what the commander asked for

Seat `fit-critic`, roster `mini-adversary`, dispatch T012, mission M001, 2026-09-20. Criterion: fit only — is this the thing the commander asked for, in the right shape, for the right system. Not correctness, not style. Every finding below is anchored on quoted text or on a command I ran read-only.

## What I grant before attacking

It runs and it is green. `/usr/bin/python3 base/bin/test_cockpit_check.py` and `test_cockpit_rename.py` both print `0 checks failed` (17 and 13 cases). `/usr/bin/python3 base/bin/cockpit-check` reports `3 id, 76 path across 25 files (tier1 37, tier2 42, tier3 0); 1 store` — the 57 advice-rename references DS006 predicted, plus real drift nobody had counted (`workshop/workshop.json` citing `crew/worker.md`, `logs/maintenance.json`, `quarters/crew/todo.txt`, and `C014`/`C015` against a manifest stopping at `C011`). The cockpit has never before been able to say that. O054's shape is delivered literally: one line on success, and each finding is `file:line kind what is wrong -> exact replacement`, fixable by a small edit with no further tool call. The commander's verbatim words are protected by construction (`orders[].text`, `*.keep`, `out-ideas/` at tier 3), which is the part of "paperwork in order" that most deserved care. The pilot authored none of it, and crew wrote directly into `base/` per D026 — the altitude in `ranks.md` was respected. None of what follows argues the work is bad. It argues it is aimed slightly off.

## The three fixes that matter most

**1. Say plainly that O045 is not delivered, and give the shapes an owner and a date.** The commander asked for "a schema store, so we can specify document shapes: stored in base, callable by a linter, so pilots have their paperwork in order" (O045). Nothing built specifies a document shape. `base/store/` holds prefixes, zones, fields and paths — a reference table, not a shape. The pilot's own framing in DS006 is honest about this ("Stage B (document shapes)"), but stage B has no spec, no schedule and no seat, while stage A occupies the commander's word: `base/store/README.md` opens "The cockpit's reference store". Cure: move stage A to `base/references/`, leave `base/store/` empty and named for the shapes, and put stage B on M001 with a date before the session log closes.

**2. Make the store fail loudly when the room move lands, not silently.** A register path that stops resolving is reported (`cockpit_store.py:230`, "register named by a prefix row does not exist"). A `fields.json` `match` glob that stops matching is reported nowhere — I grepped every `store_findings.append` in both files and there is no such check. That asymmetry bites at exactly the wrong moment: see finding F2. Cure: one store check that every `fields.json` `match` and every `zones.json` rule resolves to at least one file under `--root`, exit 2 when one does not.

**3. Put O051's register ruling in the table beside the identifier rulings it came with.** The commander ruled in the same breath: "this should probably be a universal manifest, with team numbers counted across the whole team space" and "every prefix has exactly one minting register" (O051, carried into DS006 line 85). `prefixes.json` gives `T` the register `dispatch/cockpit.json` — the per-room file whose duplication the commander was complaining about — with no `succession` row, while DS→Ds, O→Or, D→De, S→Sp, W→WS all carry one. The store's own stated invariant is one register per prefix; it records the state the commander has already ruled defective as if settled. Cure: a `succession` on the `T` row pointing at the ruled single register, or a one-line note in `prefixes.json` saying why it was left out.

## Findings, ranked

**F1 — high, fit of the idea. The commander's idea was shapes; the built thing is references, and the substitution was never put to him as a choice.** He was shown the substitution — DS006 sets out stage A and stage B, and he answered the dossier at length (O050 to O055) without objecting, and amended P013's Decision on the build's route and the lint's behaviour. So this is not something done behind him. But he was never asked the fit question directly: "your shapes idea is real; we want to do references first; is that the order you want?" He was handed an order of change already ruled. His own framing has not moved — O051, after the dossier: "currently scaffolding procedures and folder structures as part of M001". Nothing in the record shows him agreeing that a rename engine comes before his paperwork. Cure: ask him, in one line, before stage A is relied on.

**F2 — high, fit to the system it is becoming. The map is keyed on names the commander has already abolished, and the refactor it exists to enable is what expires it.** Of the 16 `match` globs in `fields.json`, every one names a path the ruled five-room sketch moves: `quarters/commander/orders.json`, `base/decisions.json`, `base/proposals.json`, `commanders-desk/in-dossiers/dossiers.json`, `missions/`, `workshop/`, `procedures/`, `dispatch/`, `logs/`. Every register path in `prefixes.json` moves too. Five of the nine rules in `zones.json` move (`notepad/**`, `logs/crew-manifest.json`, `logs/*.md`, `commanders-desk/out-ideas/**`, `commanders-desk/out-advice/**`); four survive. O052 also renames every one of those manifests to `MANIFEST-<collection>.json`. DS006's own timing puts the room move as the second commit of the same refactor flight. So the most detailed file in the store, 117 lines, is written against names with a life expectancy of one commit, and by F2's asymmetry above it will go quiet rather than red. The design does anticipate the future in places — `records/notepad/**` is already in `zones.json`, `planned` paths list `work/`, `team/`, `commander/` — which shows the team saw this and covered the cheap half. Cure: fix 2, plus a line in `base/store/README.md` naming re-keying the store as a required step of the room-move commit.

**F3 — medium, proportion. 2,915 lines against 79 findings, and the largest single piece is built for one event.** `wc -l`: `cockpit_store.py` 740, `cockpit-rename` 513, `cockpit-check` 470, the two tests 800, the store 392. `cockpit-rename` plus its test is 873 lines whose prefix machinery — succession, segments, the `superseded` state, the ordering gate, digit anchoring — exists for the identifier pass and, by its own design, refuses every prefix it knows once that pass has run (`base/store/README.md`: "the rename tool refuses it"). That is a correct design and also the admission. Within it, the tier-2 unified-diff emitter is the surface I would cut: today it produces 42 hunks that a human reads and applies by hand, for references the checker has already printed as an exact one-line replacement. The checker's finding line and the diff hunk carry the same information to the same reader. Cure: keep tier 1 and the tier counts, drop the diff emitter, let the reviewer work from `cockpit-check` output.

**F4 — medium, what would have served better. Ship the checker now; build the rename against final paths.** The smaller thing: `cockpit-check` plus the prefix, zone and path tables, roughly 1,200 lines, landed today. The pilot repairs the advice references by hand from the 37 exact replacements the checker already prints — an afternoon, against a tool that proves the result. Then the room move. Then `cockpit-rename`, built once, against paths that are final, with the identifier pass as its first job. That buys the commander his O054 lint experience on the same day, defers no correctness, and does not build a succession engine against a map due to be rewritten this week. What it costs: the identifier pass waits on the room move, reversing DS006's order. That reversal is worth putting to him, because his stated frame is folder structures first.

**F5 — medium, the assets lead. O046 was answered, not ignored, but it is going to look ignored.** The commander said "Flightcrew has these schema checking assets already built, on other branches; the spec builder was using a linter recently, along with rubric checkers." The team ran `assets-scout` on that lead and scoped flightcrew's engine to stage B, which is defensible: `schema-lib.mjs` is a JSON Schema validator with nothing to validate until shapes exist, it needs Node (a mise install, absent from a bare path), and flightcrew's path resolution is `fs.existsSync` (`assets.md` line 23), which is wrong here — this filesystem is case-insensitive and the cockpit needs case-sensitive resolution, which is `plan.md` line 50 and one of the better calls in the build. So no real duplication. The exposure is that the commander pointed at assets for the thing he asked for, and the delivered thing does not use them, because it is not that thing. F1's cure closes this too.

**F6 — low, the cockpit's own conventions in the store's own filenames.** O051: "in stream outputs `manifest.json, manifest.json and manifest.json` might be the same file or different files... `orders.json` could be a manifest file but it could also be something else." `fields.json` and `paths.json` in a streamed output say nothing about what they are. The files do carry `kind` (`cockpit-field-map`, `cockpit-path-map`), which is the other half of the ruling, and these are tables rather than manifest-and-unit collections, so the `MANIFEST-` rule does not strictly reach them. Arguable. Cure, if wanted: `cockpit-fields.json`, `cockpit-paths.json`.

**F7 — low, state shipped mid-move.** The fixture restructure landed coherently — `fixtures/stage-a/cockpit/` with its own store and a nested `base/bin/fixtures/poison/`, plus `fixtures/stage-a/broken-store/` for the exit-2 case — and both suites are green over it, so the interruption left nothing broken. Two loose ends are recorded honestly in `fixtures-report.md` and should not be lost: line 11, an open question sent to tool-builder and never answered, whether a composite `path#ID` replacement should recompute the ID half from the new basename (`DS001.md#DS001 -> CA001.md#CA001`), which is sound here only because the advice ids happen to become `CA`; and line 2, "one transient case-9 flake seen once mid-build, not reproduced". Cure: answer the composite question before the identifier pass; run the rename suite twenty times before relying on it.

**F8 — low, pace, which is the commander's own complaint.** O053: "the pilot asks for input, then four agent returns come in and the question is hundreds of lines up; the commander has to think how to solve it." O055 expected P012 to be "next session's work, depending on how this session progresses." Since then this session has produced DS006, P013 amended, a spec, a three-pass plan, two adversary rounds on the plan, the build, the fixtures, and now a mini-adversary roster on top — and the commander has not yet seen `cockpit-check` run. The work is good; the volume between his questions and his answers is the thing he already told you is too much. Cure: show him one run of the checker before the next seat is dispatched.

**F9 — a note, not a finding.** `base/store/paths.json` line 47 declares `awesome-run/` as an external root. I found no citation of it anywhere else. Harmless, but an undeclared row in a file whose whole purpose is declared rows.

## Verdict for the commander

Keep it, with changes. What was built works, catches real drift for the first time, and protects your own words carefully; the pilot's team did not overreach and did not write where it should not have.

But it is not the thing you asked for in O045. You asked for document shapes and a linter; you have a reference checker and a rename tool. Your idea is scheduled as "stage B" and currently has no spec, no date and no seat, while your word "store" has been spent on this.

Three changes before you rely on it: make stage B real on M001 with a date, so your idea is not quietly recorded as delivered; make the store fail loudly instead of going quiet when the room move breaks its map; and decide the order yourself — the identifier pass first as the dossier ruled, or the room move first, which is your own stated frame and would let the rename tool be built once against final paths.
