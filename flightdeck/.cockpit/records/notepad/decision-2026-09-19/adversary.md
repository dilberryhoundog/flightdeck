Claim attacked: line 39, "only a vehicle of 'record' dispatches crew, and everything else is written on the spot"; line 71, "The first three outputs, given the auditor's drift list, are not records."

For procedures — its own predicted majority output — it supplies trigger, writer and delivery only: no form or length spec, no source requirement, no validation, no currency rule, no commander read. `DS003.md:5` asks for the wider net ("source, library, manual, commander directives") precisely because a thin single source produced weak documents; E exempts procedures from the wide net and has them written on the spot by the pilot alone, from one source (its own session), at the moment it has just demonstrated bad judgement.
What must change: E must state a docspec, a source rule, a validation step and a currency rule for procedures, or concede that its dominant output is unspecified and that the commander's DS003 diagnosis applies to it unaltered.

## Second pass — the one-line stamp

The ruling: web-sourced records keep the current one-line `Source:` header; locally sourced records carry a one-line stamp of approval saying the document is adequately sourced, with no citations or quotes in the prose and no ledger; no extra regalia. Six findings against the replacement as the paper would have to implement it.

**F17 — Local records lose their currency trigger entirely, and the stamp is the only place left to put one. S-HIGH · C-HIGH**
Claim attacked: the paper's only currency mechanism for records was "an expiry condition" held in the withdrawn ledger (line 33). Nothing replaces it.
Evidence: `records/README.md:3` states the existing trigger — "Re-research a record when the commit or page it cites moves." That is mechanical for a web record, because the `Source:` line names a URL and a date (`records/claude-code/agent-teams.md:3`). A local record under the ruling cites nothing: no transcript refs in the prose, no ledger. So there is no artefact whose movement can be detected, and `question.md:32`'s "how it stays current" goes unanswered for exactly the class of record the commander cares most about, in a system they describe at `DS003.md:11` as "under serious construction".
What must change: the stamp line must carry a date and name the corpus it was checked against (the topic ids, the advice files, the library or manual paths), so staleness is detectable when that corpus moves. A stamp that names neither cannot be re-earned or retired.

**F18 — A stamp the writer may apply to its own work certifies nothing. S-HIGH · C-HIGH**
Claim attacked: the paper never says who applies a stamp; under E the pilot writes most things on the spot (line 39) and verifies stubs itself (line 67).
Evidence: `DS003.md:7` defines the mechanism as external by construction — "if we have a specialized team check the prose against the sources (commander transcripts, library and manuals, source html). Then we can 'stamp' them as valid." The check is by a party other than the author. This is the same defect as F13: if the pilot writes and stamps, the line records only that the pilot approves of itself, and the commander's substitute for source-in-prose has been converted into a formality that costs a line and buys nothing.
What must change: state in rule 2 that the stamp names the seat and team that applied it, that the seat did not write the document, and that the pilot may not stamp its own work; and that the stamp is not a substitute for `CLAUDE.md:24`'s commander read.

**F19 — Removing citations makes the check that earns the stamp harder, and the paper has not costed it. S-MEDIUM-HIGH · C-MEDIUM-HIGH**
Claim attacked: line 61 moves sourcing out of the prose on the grounds that it lowered prose quality; the paper treats validation as cheap thereafter.
Evidence: `DS003.md:7` names the test as "check the prose against the sources". With citations in the prose the checker verifies an anchor per claim. With the prose stripped of anchors, the checker must independently re-derive where every claim came from across the whole corpus before it can honestly write "adequately sourced". That is strictly more work, and it is team work — the paper's own named cost driver (line 23, "The cost driver is teams, not files"). E therefore attaches a team dispatch to each local record while claiming records will be rare; if the dispatch is skipped, the stamp is untrue on its face.
What must change: state what the validating seat actually does and what it costs, or concede that under the ruling the honest local record is one the pilot does not write at all.

**F20 — Edit-after-stamp survives the withdrawal; the paper's only mitigation went with the ledger. S-MEDIUM-HIGH · C-HIGH**
Claim attacked: line 80, "Separating the stamp from the prose means a record can be edited after stamping; the stamp must record what it covered"; line 61, "with the stamp naming what it stamped so a later edit cannot inherit it."
Evidence: the paper identified this risk itself and answered it only through the ledger's coverage field. Co-locating the stamp in the record file fixes discoverability, not inheritance: `CLAUDE.md:24` requires that "a record is updated in place when its topic changes", so in-place edits are the normal case, and an edited record silently carries a stamp earned by text that no longer exists.
What must change: rule 2 must say that an in-place edit voids the stamp and the record is re-stamped, and the stamp must carry the date that makes the two comparable. Without that the paper has a named, unmitigated risk.

**F21 — The form the commander wants already exists in the cockpit; inventing a new one is the regalia. S-MEDIUM · C-MEDIUM-HIGH**
Claim attacked: the implicit assumption that a new device must be designed, and any reading of the ruling under which the line says only "adequately sourced".
Evidence: `records/README.md:5` already carries a one-line local form — "Source: topic `<id>` (../logs/topics/<id>.json); commander turns at `<session>:<line>`, ...; written advice `<path>`; researched YYYY-MM-DD by `<team id>` `<seat>`." Remove that entry's last sentence (the one requiring inline per-claim citation in the body) and it is exactly the ruling: one line, at the top, naming corpus, date, team and seat, no quotes or citations in the prose, symmetrical with the web `Source:` form. A bare "adequately sourced" line names none of those, cannot be re-checked by a second reader, and is decoration asserting virtue — the thing the commander's no-regalia instruction is against.
What must change: implement the stamp as README:5's third form minus its inline-citation sentence, and say so, rather than proposing a new device.

**F22 — The change list names rule 2 only; the docspec lives in two files. S-MEDIUM · C-HIGH**
Claim attacked: line 61, "Rule 2 becomes a docspec only", with no mention of `records/README.md`.
Evidence: `records/README.md:3` independently states the requirement being removed — "The header's topic or notepad pointer is one of the six tests, and per-claim sources are another; both are required" — and `:5` defines the three Source line forms and the inline-claim rule. If only rule 2 changes, the index contradicts the docspec, and README:3's tie-break ("Where the docspec and an existing record disagree, the docspec wins") does not cover a disagreement between the docspec and the README itself. The same omission covers the stub tier: nothing in the change list tells `records/README.md` how to index a document that is not a record.
What must change: add `records/README.md:3` and `:5` to the change list, with the replacement wording.

**F23 — A stub cannot carry the stamp, and the paper gives no other marking. S-MEDIUM · C-MEDIUM**
Claim attacked: line 61, "one line permitting a record to land as a stub, marked, not citable as authority in a crew brief."
Evidence: `DS003.md:16` defines a stub as "a brief mention of existing knowledge in plain register, and a desire to improve them as work commences" — by construction not a validated document. So a stub that carried a stamp of adequate sourcing would be false on its face, and a stub without one is indistinguishable from an unstamped full record. Under the ruling the top line of a local record is now the only status signal in the file, so "marked" and "stamped" are competing for the same slot, and the paper never says what the mark is or how a reader tells the three states apart. The two-tier split is also silent on a record drawing on both web and local sources, and on whether a procedure — E's majority output — carries anything at all, which returns to F1.
What must change: define the three top-line states (stamped, stub, unstamped), say a stub never carries a stamp, and rule the mixed-source case.

## Verdict

Stands with amendments, and the second pass did not change that, though it added two more.
The routing insight is right and the evidence base is real: records were written before use, from one thin source, and delivery has never had a mechanism. E is the best option in the set.
The problem is that E is not finished. Its dominant output has no specification (F1), its central argument against the status quo rests on a failure never demonstrated (F2), and it was chosen against an option set missing the two mechanisms the scout named as closest to the problem (F3).
Those three still matter most. F1 means the commander would approve a method whose main product has no docspec, no validation and no currency rule — the conditions that produced DS002 and DS003. F2 means the case for the manifest over a CLAUDE.md condition line is unproven. F3 means harness-native memory, which costs the commander almost nothing and needs no team, was never weighed.
On the replacement, the two that matter are F17 and F18: with citations gone and no ledger, the stamp is the only place a date and a corpus can live, and without them a local record can never be re-checked or retired; and a stamp the writer may apply to itself is a line of text, not a control.
F21 is the cheapest win and the one most in the commander's spirit: the form already exists at `records/README.md:5` and needs one sentence deleted, so no new regalia has to be designed at all.
Recommend the option-maker amend rather than the commander rule: specify the procedure vehicle to the record vehicle's standard, add harness-native memory and the hook as options, withdraw "rule 4 failed", fix the rule 4 / line 37 attribution, restore the periodic read, name the rule 2 clause being removed, drop the cap cut, and define the stamp as corpus, date and stamping seat with re-stamping on edit.
Nothing here sinks the recommendation. All of it is reachable in one revision.
