## 6. What the docspec (rule 2) shows working vs. causing harm

- **Working:** the four `records/claude-code/` files are the only store in the whole cockpit with demonstrated reuse across sessions (§2) — a real catch of a false claim (`8e74fffb.md:43`) and a real arbitration of a live dispute (`8e74fffb.md:33`). These four predate the formal docspec (`workshop.json` W020 notes they "predate the docspec and lack a topic pointer") but already had its core property: one file, one topic, plain authoritative statements, no commander-quote scaffolding. That shape is what got used.
- **Working:** the "extracts, never copies" test and the "would still be true in two years" test correctly identified `lineage.md` and `commanders-guidance.md` as non-records in DS002 (`out-advice/DS002.md:30-32`, `:6-7`) before the commander even had to say so in detail — the docspec's diagnosis matched the commander's independent judgement.
- **Causing harm:** the per-claim sourcing requirement pushed citations into the prose and visibly lowered quality, exactly as the commander diagnosed (`out-advice/DS003.md:7`: "the requirement to be validated is encouraging the 'source in the prose' style for local documents, lowering the quality of the prose"). The quote-density counts in §4 are the evidence: six of seven drafts are 20-70% verbatim-quote by line, each quote necessary only to satisfy rule 2's sourcing test, not because a pilot doing work needs the commander's exact wording.
- **Causing harm:** the docspec's six tests are necessary but not sufficient — DS003 records passed line-count (all under 100), single-topic, and sourcing tests, and still five of seven were rejected as not-yet-useful or too commander-quote-heavy. The gap the docspec doesn't cover, which DS003 supplies instead, is *when* a record should be written at all (on demand vs. speculatively) and *what it draws on* (one narrow conversational source vs. a wider net of library/manual/source-HTML) — neither is a rule 2 test.
- **Causing harm:** rule 2 assumes the commander's own transcript statements are the primary source ("every claim names its source... a branch, commit and researcher" — `CLAUDE.md:24`), which structurally produces the harness-register-vs-six-quote-records split seen in §4: the one non-commander-sourced, docs-verified record is the one that survived whole.
