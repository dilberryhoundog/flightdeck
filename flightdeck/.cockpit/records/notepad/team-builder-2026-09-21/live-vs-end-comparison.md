## 3. What end-only found that eleven live rounds missed, and why

- **The paper's own factual furniture.** B5, B6, B9, B13 and B3 are each one command: a `lessons` sweep, a directory listing, a `grep -l`, a `model:` line, a schema read. The live pair attacked whatever was newly in front of them — a landing evidence report, then a numbered pass — and incidental facts entered the paper from sources both had already cleared in an earlier round. A cold reader has no cleared list.
- **Two commander citations resolving to the wrong file (A23, B12), both endorsed by the live adversary in round 10.** It checked that the quote was verbatim and the claim true; it did not check that the named file contained it, because it had watched the claim arrive through the miner's C-list and carried the miner's provenance in its head. This is the live mode's characteristic blindness and it is the mirror of its strength.
- **B1, the residual the live adversary itself requested.** An author cannot attack his own correction. A fresh reader went to `record-mining.md:121`, read the column and found the seven never held M05 or M13.
- **The axis clauses read as a rubric (A2 to A7, A17 to A19).** Eleven live rounds attacked evidence, premises and sources; nobody applied topic 1's six clauses as the paper's own heading demands. The live seat saw each clause arrive as the fix to a finding it had raised, so each clause carried its own justification with it. A cold reader sees only the clause.
- The same freshness misfires: A16 is a cold path check generalised wrongly, and B7 a cold settings read with the project's own settings file unlooked-for.

## 5. Verdict against the commander's two extremes

It did not obstruct in his sense. No seat stopped or was interrupted: the live seat reported to the lead alone, the lead relayed once per round, and every crew member it attacked was idle when the findings landed. But work was redone — one pass of topic 4 — so obstruction appeared not as interruption but as rework arriving through lateness, and the paper's line 138 claims both halves when only the first was proved (F62).

It was not silent. Findings reached the paper-holder before pass 2 was written and every pass from 3 on was attacked before the next began. The pilot's "slid toward silence" reading is disproved by the transcript: rounds 2 and 3 were taken back to back as the landing notices arrived. What survives is narrower — the seat closed rounds with "standing by" instead of listing the folder, so it depended on notices reaching it (F63).

Value fell in the middle and nearer the obstruction end than the silent one, and the cost sat on the lead rather than the crew. Four findings changed an answer the commander would otherwise have acted on (F51, F52, F55, F59) and three wrong headlines were stopped short of the dossier (E38's ranking, F22's own claim, the origin sort) — none of which a pass-7 reader could reach.

The tandem paid for itself: the second adversary found the first agreeing with its own source, forcing a withdrawal the first reached later only by reading the other's file; no finding of one duplicated or contradicted the other. Its cost was the late declaration of the division, not the second seat.

What this flight cannot show: n=1, and there is no control — this paper was never written with the attack held to the end, so the counterfactual is unmeasured. The pool is a stand-in, not a control: it read a paper the live rounds had already repaired seven times, so its 42 findings measure what survives live attack, not what end-only attack would have found on an unattacked paper. And the obstruction result could not have been observed under this design, since no working seat ever saw the adversary (A19).
