# XX-006 — The constitution limits the pilot's bash, CO-089 removes it

One side comes out of `cockpit.constitution` lines 62, 63 and 143. The other is the commander's order CO-089, amended by him on 2026-09-25 to grant grep, which closes the grep half of this dispute; the bash half stands.

## CO-102 and CO-109

"Bash - Restricted through pilot definition. pre-approved commands excepted (claude -p)" [62]

"Grepping - restricted. Too much token bulk inside the main session." [63]

"The pilot can write small changes through using claude -p with the appropriate flags." [143]

## CO-089

"The Pilot does not use bash tools. Some may be granted back later. The pilot reads, writes, edits, greps and globs."

## The contradiction

On bash the two records differ: CO-102 restricts it but excepts pre-approved commands and names `claude -p` as one; CO-089 says the pilot does not use bash tools, with nothing excepted and anything granted back only later. CO-109, which relied on the exception, was retired by the commander on 2026-09-25, so the only record still naming `claude -p` is CO-102's exception clause. CO-089 is the later record and reads as the commander tightening the rule until he grants commands back. On grep the records now agree: CO-102 calls it restricted and CO-089, as amended, grants it. What remains is narrow: whether CO-102's `claude -p` exception still means anything while CO-089 stands.
