# Decision research spec — [decision name]

Status: draft

## Intent

The decision this research serves, who makes it, and by when. State why it cannot be made on what is already known, and what it costs to make it unresearched or late. The picture of success is not an answer but a state: the decision-maker can choose between the options from the report alone, and can say which finding each part of the choice rests on. Keep it short enough to judge every finding against.

## Scope

The perimeter around the question. Name the options under consideration and the ones deliberately excluded; the kinds of source in and out; the depth expected — a survey of what exists, or verification of specific claims; and the adjacent questions that look related and are not being asked. State whether the report may recommend or must stop at evidence, since the two produce different documents. The perimeter constrains what may be investigated, never what may be found.

## Constraints

The conditions imported from outside: source policy (recency cutoff, licensing, confidentiality, which sources are authoritative for which kind of claim), the citation standard, the time-box and source budget that define when research has stalled, report length limits, and where the report is stored. Write each so it can be checked — a date, a count, a format that validates.

## Interfaces and contracts

The exact shape of the report, fixed before research begins. A finding is one cited claim answering part of a numbered question, and the template states the fields every finding carries — claim, source, date checked, confidence and its basis. An unresolved question is recorded in its own field with the reason and the sources consulted, never omitted. The matrix is the options-by-criteria table the decision is read from: its rows and columns are fixed here, so that research fills cells rather than inventing axes. State the report template as a skeleton file, the citation format exactly, and the source list schema if sources are registered separately.

## Behaviours

The numbered questions the decision turns on, one per entry, each written as what an acceptable answer must contain rather than what the answer is. Typical entries: the report states, for every option, whether it satisfies the criterion, with a primary-source citation dated within the cutoff; the report states the cost of each option in the unit the constraint names; every cell of the matrix is filled or marked unresolved. Number the report's own behaviours too — every claim carries a citation, every citation resolves, the matrix contains exactly the fixed options and criteria.

## Edge cases and failure modes

The outcomes when evidence does not cooperate, decided in advance: two credible sources that contradict each other; no source for a cell; a question that turns out to be ill-posed once investigated; an option outside scope that the evidence keeps pointing at; a source behind a login or paywall; a criterion that cannot be evidenced from any permitted source. Each gets an exact return — a conflict is recorded with both sources and no adjudication; an out-of-scope option is named in unresolved, not researched — and none is resolved by inference dressed as a finding.

## Prior decisions and non-goals

What is already known and must not be re-investigated: facts established, options already ruled out and why, sources already judged authoritative or unreliable, positions the organisation has taken. The non-goals: the research does not make the decision, does not widen the option list, and does not revisit criteria once fixed. Lessons from earlier research runs — where reports drifted into advocacy, where citations went stale — belong here until they become standing rules.

## Verification

Named checks with an unarguable pass or fail, mapped to behaviour numbers: the report validates against the template; every matrix cell is filled or marked unresolved; every claim carries a citation; every citation resolves; every cited date is within the cutoff; the option and criterion sets match the ones fixed here exactly. One check needs a human and is declared as such: a sample of citations, of stated size, read against the claims they support, with a rubric of binary questions. The end-to-end proof is the decision-maker, reading the report and nothing else, able to state which finding each part of the choice rests on.

## Definition of done

Every named check passed with its output presented; the citation sample passed at its rubric; the independent review returned no open finding on scope or on whether the report answers the decision in intent; the unresolved count is stated on the report's first page; nothing outside the option and criterion perimeter was researched; the report is delivered at the constrained location. The decision itself is not a condition here — it belongs to the human, after the gate.

## Open Questions

Empty at the freeze. Until then, every question the author deferred and every finding the attacker filed, verbatim, each phrased as a decision with options.
